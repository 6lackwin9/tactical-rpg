# Campaign structure: checkpoints and forks

Status: canon (user 2026-10-25). Game-agnostic. How a campaign gives the player freedom (optional missions, forks,
exploring) while the story still lands on time. A game's version lives in its canon sheet (meters and act lines).

## Checkpoints

- Each act names a few checkpoint missions, each with a day deadline on the campaign clock.
- Missing a checkpoint gives the act a bad end: the cell could not prevent the events. Decide per act whether a bad end
  stops the run (a bad-end card, reload the act's start) or lets the story go on worse (lasting costs).
- Deadlines leave enough time to finish the act in the worst case (every fight lost once, every rest taken); the
  balance lane checks this with the sim.

## Forks converge

- Every mission converges on the same climax. Optional missions, side quests and forks are free as long as the
  checkpoints are met; branches change costs, allies and flags, not the act's destination.
- A reveal may lock side quests that no longer make sense after it, so the player weighs advancing the story against
  exploring. Lock only side quests, never a checkpoint.

## Meters across act lines

- A meter that carries across acts keeps a floor (the world remembers), so a new act starts with less room and the
  player has to manage it.
- A clock's true deadline may be revealed mid-campaign; the reveal corrects the visible clock once, and what is left
  becomes the next act's budget, so early planning pays off.

## Losses and retries

- Early losses cost time and condition, not progress (retreat, wounded, the mission stays).
- A retry of a climactic fight needs a reason inside the story, and each retry costs something the player can see.
