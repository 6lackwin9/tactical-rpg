Emberwake: A Scarlet Lullaby - Flow Music brief (ART_UI_STUDIO 3.9)

Tool: Flow Music (flowmusic.app, from Flow's top bar). A chat "Producer" on Lyria 3.5 that makes and edits tracks;
downloads are free. Paste SOUND BIBLE below into Customize Producer > memories once, so every session keeps it.

================================================================ SOUND BIBLE (paste into Producer memories)
All music is for "Emberwake: A Scarlet Lullaby", a dark-fantasy tactical RPG in a soot-black industrial-gothic city
(the Sunk Ward): tenement rooftops, tallow vats, bone-mills, iron cages, cathedral bells, rain and searchlights. A
theocracy tithes its youths to keep a dreaming titan asleep; a small cell of young deserters fights back.

- Always instrumental unless asked otherwise. Never lyrics. Wordless voices only when asked.
- Palette: low strings (cello, double bass, viola), a solo violin or viola da gamba, hurdy-gurdy, music box and
  celesta, low male choir and a lone child's or woman's wordless voice, church bells and hand bells, struck metal
  (anvil, chains, iron pipes), frame drums and taiko-like low drums, harp, dulcimer, a small pipe organ.
- Avoid: modern pop, EDM, trap, rock drum kits, electric guitar, synth leads, epic trailer "braams", happy major keys.
- Mood: cold, sooty, sorrowful, defiant; tension held, not released; warmth is rare and earned (the hearth).
- The leitmotif "The Scarlet Lullaby": a simple, slightly sad 6/8 cradle tune that circles in D minor (Dorian
  colour) and never fully resolves. The Clerisy sings it to the tithe-children; it returns in many tracks: sweet on a
  music box, cold in the choir, broken on a hurdy-gurdy in battle, warm on the hearth's strings.
- Game use: tracks loop in the game, so keep each one in a single steady mood and tempo with no fade-out ending,
  no big intro build and no outro; 90 to 150 seconds is enough.

================================================================ TEST 1 - main theme, two directions (pick by ear)
Make two versions; the winner sets the sound for every other track.

1A (dark acoustic, cinematic):
Main menu theme for Emberwake: A Scarlet Lullaby. Instrumental. It opens on a music box playing the Scarlet
Lullaby, a simple sad 6/8 cradle tune in D minor that circles without resolving; low cello and a soft distant church
bell join under it, then a slow low male choir hums the same tune, cold and solemn, over rain and a faint crackle of
tallow fire. 66 BPM. No drums, no big build. Steady mood throughout so it loops; no fade-out ending. About 2 minutes.

1B (16-bit era, retro console):
Main menu theme for Emberwake: A Scarlet Lullaby, in the style of a 16-bit SNES-era dark fantasy RPG soundtrack:
sampled orchestra, slightly lo-fi, warm and grainy. Instrumental. A music box plays the Scarlet Lullaby, a simple sad
6/8 cradle tune in D minor that circles without resolving; sampled strings and a choir pad carry it, with a slow
church bell. 66 BPM. No modern drums. Steady mood throughout so it loops; no fade-out ending. About 2 minutes.

================================================================ TEST 2 - hub (the Sluice Undercroft, the hearth)
Hub theme for a dark-fantasy RPG's underground refuge: a flooded stone undercroft lit by a charcoal forge, water
dripping into a basin. Instrumental, in the same sound as the main theme. A warm, quiet variation of the Scarlet
Lullaby on solo viola and harp, with a low cello drone, soft crackling coals and slow dripping water; the only warm
place in a cold city: tired, safe for now, still sad. 72 BPM, 6/8. No drums. Steady mood so it loops; no ending.
About 2 minutes.

================================================================ TEST 3 - battle (regular fights)
Battle theme for a dark-fantasy tactical RPG, turn-based fights in soot-black alleys and on rooftops. Instrumental,
in the same sound as the main theme. Driving low frame drums and taiko-like hits at 120 BPM, staccato low strings,
struck metal and chains as percussion, a hurdy-gurdy playing a broken, faster fragment of the Scarlet Lullaby in
D minor, short choir stabs. Tense and determined, not heroic. Steady intensity so it loops; no intro build, no
ending. About 2 minutes.
