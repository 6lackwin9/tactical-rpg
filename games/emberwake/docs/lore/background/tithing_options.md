# The Great Tithing: story options

Status: proposal (2026-10-25). Options for the user; nothing here is canon until picked. The user accepted Q-04 and
Q-16 in principle (a yearly rite of hundreds plus a Great Tithing of thousands; recurring lesser tithings when the soft
Doom Clock hits 0) and asked for the story around them: do commoners know only the smaller tithings, why is the Great
Tithing needed now, and is it the first or does it have a precedent. All options follow the
[central truth](central_truth.md) option A (Q-01, canon: the lullaby is a harvest). No new character is named. Part of
the [story guide](../STORY_GUIDE.md) (gap G-24).

## 2. Shared by every option: the clock and the lesser tithings

- Why now, in the open: the titan's tremors are rising (the Awakening Fissure under the Sluice in the Act 1 interlude
  is the cell's first sight of it), and the Clerisy blames the titan's restlessness for the folk's woes. Why now, in
  truth: the dream is thinning, so less marrow rises through the conduits; Othemar, 64 and half calcified, needs one
  harvest large enough to last.
- Acts 1-2, soft clock at 0: the Clerisy holds a lesser tithing (hundreds or fewer, taken from the pens) to keep the
  dream fed while the pens fill for the Great one, and the clock restarts at a new count (days: balance lane). Story
  cost: any named stake not yet freed is taken (story guide G-20: Pip, Ida), and the freed-youth count drops (Q-29).
  Tithings out of season are themselves a sign that something is wrong.
- Mission 2.4: the documents give the Great Tithing's date and scale; the clock is corrected once and becomes the Act 3
  budget (Q-17 option a); it turns hard in Act 3 and stops when Othemar falls at node 21.
- The options differ in what commoners know, the precedent, and how the player learns it.

## 3. Option A: a secret, and the first ever

- Commoners know: only the yearly rite. The rite "in six weeks" is the yearly one to them, to the cell and on the clock.
- Why now: Othemar's own hunger. The yearly rites still feed the dream, but no longer enough to keep him alive; the
  Great Tithing is his invention, called his last sacrament. The Clerisy's doctrine never asked for it.
- Precedent: none. "Each generation" is pious language for the yearly rites.
- The player learns: mission 2.4 (the documents: thousands, and the date), Act 3 (Malachi explains why the dream needs
  it, Q-02 rec).
- Doom Clock: as section 2; the clock is labelled "the Red Tithing" until 2.4, then "the Great Tithing".
- Endings: A Preservation ends a harvest that has just escalated; B Ascendancy puts the cell in the seat of an elite
  that was ready to take thousands; C Red Martyrdom answers the largest Tithing with a willing one.
- Cost and risk: the cheapest (no history to write; nothing changes in Act 1). The reveal is about scale only, and
  Maren's texts (Q-07), Agnes's tales and the Reliquary tomb (Q-12) stay without a payoff.

## 4. Option B: a secret, with a hidden precedent

- Commoners know: only the yearly rite, as in A.
- Why now: the last Great Tithing, two centuries ago, sated the dream for two centuries; it is thinning again, so the
  Clerisy's own chronicles call for another (a cycle of one known turn, set by the dream, not by the calendar).
- Precedent: the last Great Tithing was held in the dark of the Extinguishing, two centuries ago, and its dead were
  recorded as victims of the titan's wrath; the public story blames the horror, as it does today. Sub-choice: (b1) the
  Great Tithing caused the Shroud; (b2) it only used the Extinguishing as cover. Recommendation: b2 (the Shroud stays a
  mystery and the cosmology does not change).
- The player learns: Act 1, a first hint in Agnes's tales of the stars and in what Maren remembers of the forbidden
  texts (Q-07 option a: they are the Clerisy's chronicle of the last Great Tithing); mission 2.4, the date and scale;
  mission 2.6, the Reliquary tomb holds the record (Q-12 option a: "the first Extinguishers" are the clerics who held
  that Great Tithing); Act 3, Malachi confirms what the dream is fed for.
- Doom Clock: as section 2. The Extinguished failure screen becomes a real echo: losing means a second Extinguishing
  for the Unsealed.
- Endings: A Preservation ends the cycle for good (no dream to feed); B Ascendancy hands the cell the chronicle and the
  source, so the next Great Tithing would be theirs to call; C Red Martyrdom sates the dream so the cycle never turns
  again.
- Cost and risk: more history to keep consistent; pays off four open threads already in the docs (Agnes, Q-07, Q-12,
  the Extinguished echo). If Maren is the captured founder, her Act 1 hint is lost, so 2.4 and 2.6 must carry the
  reveal alone.

## 5. Option C: proclaimed in public

- Commoners know: the Clerisy proclaims from day 1 that this year's rite is a Great Tithing, because the titan stirs.
  The city counts down with the cell; frightened families hand over children for salt.
- Why now: the tremors, as the public reason; the thinning dream, as the hidden one.
- Precedent: the Clerisy claims an ancient one (true or false, the player cannot tell until Act 3).
- The player learns: the size from the hearth scene (Vesper's line names the Great Tithing); the true reason at 2.4 and
  from Malachi in Act 3.
- Doom Clock: labelled "the Great Tithing" from day 1; at 0 in Acts 1-2 the Clerisy postpones it (the pens are not
  full) and holds a lesser tithing instead; 2.4 gives the real date.
- Endings: as A, played to a city that watched it coming.
- Cost and risk: removes the Act 2 discovery of scale; changes the hearth line and the vignette; makes commoners active
  in the Great Tithing, which cuts against the drugged-and-docile Spent; goes against Q-04's own recommendation to keep
  game text qualitative.

## 6. Recommendation

Option B with b2. It answers the user's framing (commoners know only the yearly rite; the Great Tithing is the
climax the clock builds toward), gives the Great Tithing a reason grounded in the central truth (the dream thins), and
turns the Clerisy's habit of blaming the titan into a historical lie the cell uncovers step by step. It pays off
threads the docs already hold without new characters. Option A is the fallback: same mechanics, no history.

| Reveal at 2.4 | scale and date | scale, date, and the cycle | real date and reason |
| New history | none | the last Great Tithing two centuries ago | a claimed precedent |
| Open threads paid off | none | Agnes, Q-07, Q-12, the Extinguished echo | none |
| Docs touched | few | most Tithing docs | hearth, vignette, Act 1 |
